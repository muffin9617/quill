How to Evaluate a GEO Agency Beyond AI Visibility

Evaluate GEO providers through customer questions, answer accuracy, content quality, measurement methods and clear delivery responsibilities.

HSHan ShenB2B Digital & AI Marketing Architect 2026-09-09 About 4 min read

Visibility is an observation that needs interpretation

Generative engine optimization providers may report how often a brand appears in a set of prompts or questions. These observations help teams understand how AI answers present the brand. A mention or a higher position still requires further evidence before drawing conclusions about customer understanding, purchase intent or revenue.

Evaluation should examine whether the questions reflect real buying decisions, whether answers are accurate, whether content and source strategies are credible, and how the work informs subsequent business improvements.

Examine performance-based pricing carefully

In a conversation with a provider, I heard a proposal to charge according to visibility against agreed prompts. Payment would apply to the questions where the agreed display condition was met. Before signing such an agreement, several definitions deserve attention.

  • How are questions chosen, and do they cover real customer roles and decision stages?
  • What qualifies as an appearance? How are a brand name, product name and accurate solution recommendation treated?
  • Are results comparable across AI products, versions, regions and dates?
  • What work did the provider perform, and what changed elsewhere in the company’s content or brand activity?
  • Did customers receive accurate and useful information when the visibility condition was achieved?

Pricing terms can allocate commercial risk. Their usefulness depends on whether the measured outcome reflects something valuable to the customer.

Review the work behind the report

GEO services may include question research, planning, content production, external distribution, monitoring and review. Assess deliverables and ownership at each stage.

Research into customer questions

The provider should explain the needs behind each question and its relationship to buying roles, awareness stages and procurement tasks. A large set of popular prompts may have limited relevance to the company’s actual customers.

Content and source strategy

Check whether content uses accurate product information, customer evidence and professional judgment. Distribution channels should be relevant and suitable for public use. Establish which material needs company approval and which sources support the claims.

Execution and governance

Assign responsibility for fact-checking, brand approval, copyright and permission to use customer information. Establish how content will be updated and errors corrected. These responsibilities shape long-term credibility.

Monitoring and interpretation

Reports should record the product or model, date, region, question set and sampling method. They should also examine whether mentions are accurate, sources are credible and product capabilities are represented correctly. Reproducible methods make comparisons more useful.

Measure across four levels

  1. Coverage. Does the brand or product appear for questions relevant to target customers?
  2. Accuracy. Are positioning, capabilities, use cases and limitations represented correctly?
  3. Content quality. Do answers and citations help customers understand a problem, compare options or validate a claim?
  4. Business feedback. Do relevant customers visit, inquire, enter sales conversations or reveal new needs?

Coverage and accuracy are useful diagnostic measures. Business feedback is closer to commercial outcomes, while also being influenced by sales cycles, other touchpoints and sample size. Interpret the levels together.

Eight questions before signing

  • Where do the target prompts come from, and who approves their business relevance?
  • How do you distinguish a mention from an accurate recommendation?
  • Do you record the model, date, region, prompt wording and version?
  • How do you handle different answers to repeated questions?
  • What exactly is included in content production and external distribution?
  • How do you obtain permission and verify customer cases, data and expert views?
  • How do you report negative, inaccurate or outdated brand information?
  • Which outcomes depend on your work, and which depend on our approvals, materials and website updates?

For an offer with a performance guarantee, document the definition, method, observation period, exclusions and remedy if the target is missed. Research, content and monitoring are deliverables the provider can manage. Model answers and downstream revenue also depend on external conditions.

Where a provider can add value

A provider can save research and execution time, establish monitoring, improve content governance and coordinate channels. These capabilities can matter for a team building its first GEO process.

Examine whether the work improves understanding of customer questions, identifies public information gaps, produces material sales teams can reuse and changes the feedback received from target customers. Automated visibility reports alone leave these questions open.

Improve the information customers can use

GEO evaluation should connect the importance of the question to answer accuracy, source credibility and customer judgment. As measurement methods and behavior evolve, review visibility alongside accuracy, question coverage and business feedback.

A valuable service helps the brand be understood correctly and improves the information customers use to make decisions.
